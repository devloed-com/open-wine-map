# Delimitation sweep — how precisely the non-French specifications delimit their area (2026-09-23)

Scope: every non-French record whose delimitation text carries a cadastral, exclusion or partial-municipality marker, or that overlaps another appellation of its country beyond a nested (regional-over-local) relationship — 849 records read in 91 batches by one reader each; every record the reader rated *high* was re-read by a second agent against the full text (135 verified, 69 corrected). France is out of scope (the INAO parcellaire already gives parcel precision). The texts are the extracted `geo_area` section of the EU single document, the MASAF disciplinare article 3 for Italy, or the national specification.

Basis legend: **municipality-list** whole administrative units only · **partial-municipality** only part of a municipality, with the boundary described · **cadastral-units** explicit cadastral identifiers · **named-sites** lieux-dits / parajes / località / Rieden / dűlők · **altitude-limit** · **natural-boundary** rivers, ridges, coasts · **map-annex** the document defers to an annexed map.

## What the sweep found

**Overlapping specifications almost never reference each other.** The readers checked 2,338 overlap partners named in the record headers; a document explicitly addresses the shared border for 104 of them (Italy 74 of 1,479, Portugal 13 of 213, Spain 6 of 256, Slovenia 6 of 29, Cyprus 2 of 10). Where an overlap *is* resolved, it is resolved by each document's own sub-municipal line, not by naming the neighbour: Castel del Monte and Moscato di Trani both cut Trani, Andria and Corato along the SP 231; the two Lambrusco DOCs split the comune of Modena with a word-for-word identical trace; Menfi takes only fogli 1–7, 10, 11, 13–16 and 23 of Sciacca; the two Krasochoria Lemesou PDOs split Omodos at the Cha-Potami river, each claiming one bank. The rest of the 5,160 same-country overlaps are either whole-municipality lists that genuinely share municipalities (the Italian DOC families, the Portuguese regional IGPs) or narrative traces that split a municipality without saying which appellation is on the other side.

**Three kinds of sub-municipal precision exist in the texts, and they map to very different open data.**

1. **Cadastral identifiers — 99 records.** Spain (50): the polígono / parcela lists of the Vinos de Pago estates (Abadía Retuerta, Dominio de Valdepusa, Finca Élez, Guijoso, Dehesa del Carrizal, Pago Florentino, Vallegarcia, Chozas Carrascal, Los Balagueses, El Terrerazo, Vera de Estenas, Calzadilla, Urueña, Rosalejo, Dehesa Peñalba, Bolandín, Aylés, Tharsys, Urbezo, La Jaraba, Los Cerrillos, El Vicario, Campo de la Guardia, Campo de Calatrava, Río Negro, Sierra Sur de Jaén …) and the polígono-level inclusions or exclusions inside DO municipios (La Mancha, Rueda, Arlanza, Campo de Borja, Valdejalón, Ribera del Gállego-Cinco Villas, Cataluña, Tarragona, Conca de Barberà, Penedès, Montsant, Priorat, Uclés, Méntrida, Arribes, the eight Alicante subzones). All of it resolves against SIGPAC, whose polígono and recinto geometry is open data and whose download is already wired for one comarca. Italy (20): cadastral fogli and particelle — Menfi / Sciacca, Colline Joniche Tarantine (42 references), Conegliano-Valdobbiadene (the Rive and Cartizze fogli), Cinque Terre, the Valpolicella family, Soave Superiore and Recioto di Soave, Valtellina Superiore, Alto Adige, Trentino, Teroldego Rotaliano, Eloro, Delia Nivolelli, Casteggio, Asolo Montello, Collio Goriziano — resolvable against the Agenzia delle Entrate cadastral map (WMS, and open downloads per foglio since 2021; licence to confirm). Slovenia (12) and Slovakia (7): boundaries run along named katastrske občine / katastrálne územia, often combined with contour lines; both countries publish the cadastral-municipality layer openly (e-prostor, ZBGIS). Germany (8): Gemarkung / Flur / Flurstück lists for the six Einzellage PDOs, Großräschener See, Sächsischer Landwein, Saale-Unstrut and parts of Württemberg — ALKIS is open in Brandenburg, Saxony and Saxony-Anhalt, not in Rhineland-Palatinate or Baden-Württemberg.

2. **Sub-municipal administrative units — the largest class outside Italy.** Portuguese cadernos delimit by freguesia (43 records), Romanian caiete by sat / localitate (34), Greek and Cypriot specifications by κοινότητα (35 + 3), Bulgarian ones by the землища of settlements (the readers first called these cadastral; the verifiers corrected them to administrative), Italian disciplinari by frazione. Where an open boundary layer exists at that level the gain is mechanical: DGT CAOP carries every Portuguese freguesia and is already on disk; GISCO LAU is already at community level for Greece and Cyprus; Slovenia's and Slovakia's cadastral municipalities are open. Romanian sate and Italian frazioni have no official open polygon layer (ISTAT's località abitate census polygons are the nearest thing for Italy).

3. **Narrative boundary lines and altitude limits — Italy's default.** 278 Italian disciplinari include municipalities only in part and trace the internal line along roads, rivers, ridges and contour lines; 264 of them are in principle re-drawable from OSM roads and rivers, 157 involve an altitude threshold, and 58 state one outright (Alto Adige above / below 650 m at Scena, Valpolicella, Custoza …). Greece adds 29 altitude limits. This is digitising work, not parsing: a road-by-road trace is a person with a map, though a pure altitude limit can be applied mechanically with a DEM as a display refinement of the zone. About 80 records (Greece 29, Portugal 15, Italy 11, Cyprus 10, Spain 9, Germany 4) defer to an annexed map as the authority and give nothing else to parse.

**Reading quality.** Every record the readers rated *high* was re-read by a second agent against the full text; 69 of 135 came back corrected, mostly on two grounds — quoted references that differ from the source only by a PDF line wrap (harmless) and priority downgrades where the text splits a municipality without naming the neighbouring appellation (Castelli Romani, Dolcetto d'Asti, Frascati) or lists settlement territories rather than cadastral units (Asenovgrad, Yuzhno Chernomorie). Medium and low rows carry a single reader's judgement. The per-record classification, the overlap pairs and the source texts are in `tmp/delimitation-sweep-2026-09-23/`.
