# Fact check of the 200 most visited appellations (2026-09-20)

Scope: the 200 appellations with the most `Appellation Viewed` panel opens plus entity-page pageviews in Plausible (site `openwinemap.com`, all time; Bourgogne leads with 656, rank 200 has 46). For each record every terroir-fact bullet as rendered on the English card was graded against the exact regulator text stage 02d extracted from (`_lib/terroir_sources`) and the Wikipedia hints, together with its source-language bullet and grounding quotes; the card header (classification, region, styles, grape list) was checked for contradictions with the same sources. Grading was done by 20 Claude subagents reading the packets directly, not through the Anthropic API (`audit_terroir_facts_llm.py` was not run). Raw verdicts: `tmp/terroir-facts-review/fact-check-top200-2026-09-20.json`.

## What the misleading bullets are

**29 of 37 misleading bullets are sibling-zone leakage on sub-denomination cards.** A DGC card inherits the parent's bullet list, and the parent's cahier describes each DGC in turn, so a bullet extracted from the *Castillon* or *Sainte-Foy* paragraph is rendered on the *Blaye* and *Cadillac* cards as if it described them. Stage 04's sibling filter only drops a bullet that *leads with* another sub-denomination's name; these bullets name no zone at all. Affected in the top 200: cotes-de-bordeaux-blaye (9 of 15 bullets), cotes-de-bordeaux-cadillac (7 of 15), rioja-rioja-alta (3 of 6 describe Rioja Alavesa), bourgogne-cote-d-or / bourgogne-hautes-cotes-de-nuits / bourgogne-hautes-cotes-de-beaune (2 each: the Yonne–Châtillonnais cuestas and the Saône-et-Loire granite soils), bourgogne-cote-chalonnaise (1), languedoc-sommieres (1, a La Méjanelle sentence), rose-d-anjou (2, the Anjou mousseux history from the shared Anjou cahier). The same mechanism produces the header contradictions on Blaye, Cadillac, Saumur Puy-Notre-Dame and Mâcon-Villages (styles and grapes inherited from the parent although the cahier restricts the DGC to reds, or to whites).

The remaining misleading bullets are isolated, one per record:

- **#3 coteaux-champenois** (bullet 1, translation; mistranslation, invented-detail): 'le Vitryat marnais' means the Vitryat of the Marne département, not 'the marly Vitryat'; the English invents a soil property (marne/marly) that the source does not state.
- **#11 beaujolais** (bullet 6, translation; mistranslation): The cahier says short pruning is used 'afin de maitriser la fertilité de ce cépage' (fruitfulness/yield), and the SRC keeps 'fertilité'; the English says 'to control its vigour', a different viticultural property.
- **#51 cataluna** (bullet 7, extraction; narrowed-attribution, unsupported-causal-link): The source credits the refreshing sensation to three factors combined — "una graduación alcohólica menor y una mayor acidez, que unidas a la presencia de dióxido de carbono endógeno, aportan la sensación refrescante" — while the bullet (already in the Spanish SRC) attributes it to the CO2 alone.
- **#81 saint-pourcain** (bullet 3, translation; mistranslation, wrong-direction): The cahier says 'Les coteaux sur substratum argilo-calcaire orientés est/sud-est', i.e. slopes *facing* east/south-east; the English 'clay-limestone hillsides to the east and south-east', in parallel with the granitic hillsides 'to the west', turns an exposure into a location.
- **#132 cannonau-di-sardegna** (bullet 5, translation; wrong-entity, mistranslation): The Wikipedia hint reads "un visitatore del re, Martin Carrillo" — Carrillo is the king's visitor, not a king; the English "a visitor of King Martín Carrillo" makes Carrillo a monarch.
- **#163 franken** (bullet 3, both; wrong-entity, mistranslation): The source contrasts the two: 'Während die Verebnungsflächen, die mainfränkischen Platten, ... überwiegend landwirtschaftlich genutzt werden, stehen auf den Böden des Buntsandsteins, des Muschelkalkes und des Keupers Reben' — the vines are NOT on the loess-covered plateaux; the bullet places them there.
- **#145 cremant-de-die** (bullet 6, extraction; wrong-entity): the 1910 decree and the 1942 AOC belong to Clairette de Die (same cahier); Crémant de Die was only separated in 1993, so the card dates the appellation to 1910/1942.
- **#174 cote-de-beaune-villages** (bullet 4, extraction; other, added-qualifier): The cahier attaches the gradient figures to one soil type only — "Les coteaux au substrat marneux présentent souvent une pente moyenne de 20%, pouvant aller jusqu'à 40 %" — whereas the bullet gives 20% as the average gradient of all delimited plots and adds an unsupported "steeper".

## Header contradictions by class

- 12 × region label SUD-OUEST on a Bordeaux appellation (INAO comité régional, not a wine region)
- 11 × principal / accessory role contradicts the source
- 11 × style category missing or wrongly present (sparkling / liqueur / sweet / rosé)
- 8 × grape named by the source missing from the list
- 6 × sub-denomination / shared-cahier header inherited from the parent
- 6 × style missing
- 5 × rosé / gris style missing
- 3 × VIFA experimental varieties (arinarnoa, castets, marselan, touriga nacional, vidoc) listed as principal
- 3 × other
- 2 × grape list empty
- 2 × garbled grape entry
- 1 × region covers only one of two regions (Prosecco)

Verified against the data:

- **Region = SUD-OUEST on every Bordeaux appellation** (32 FR records corpus-wide; 11 in the top 200: Haut-Médoc, Margaux, Saint-Julien, Saint-Émilion grand cru, Bordeaux supérieur, Cadillac, Listrac, Moulis, Lalande-de-Pomerol, Graves supérieures, Côtes de Bordeaux Blaye/Cadillac). The value is the SIQO `comite_regional` column, which INAO uses for its regional committee, not a wine region — a labelling choice rather than an extraction error, but no reader files Pomerol under Sud-Ouest.
- **Empty grape lists on two top-150 cards.** Costières de Nîmes (#26): the 2025 republication puts a conformity paragraph before `1°- Encépagement` inside section V and the parser finds nothing. Picpoul de Pinet (#149): section V is missing altogether from the extracted sections (IV jumps to VI), so the single variety piquepoul blanc is never read.
- **Garbled grape entry.** Pacherenc du Vic-Bilh (#119) lists `suivie-de-la-manseng` as a principal grape: the cahier's two-column table interleaves `AOC « Pacherenc du Vic-Bilh » suivie de la mention « sec »` with the variety cells. Valais (#100) carries a stray `blanche` and comma-suffixed entries from the règlement scan.
- **Shared cahier.** Graves supérieures (#180) is bound to the same PDF as Graves, so the sweet-white-only appellation shows red style and six red varieties as principal.
- **VIFA varieties as principal** (Haut-Médoc, Bordeaux supérieur, Listrac; the corpus-wide Bordeaux cahiers): arinarnoa, castets, marselan, touriga nacional, vidoc are 'variétés d'intérêt à fin d'adaptation' capped at 5 % and 10 % of the blend, extracted with role `principal`.
- **Styles under-reported** on 16 cards, mostly rosé/gris (Vinho Verde, Porto, Oolde, Ambt Delden, Achterhoek, Crémant d'Alsace, Côtes de Toul, Châteaumeillant, Prosecco, Terre Alfieri white) and sparkling / liqueur categories the single document lists (Baden, Ahr, Pico, Alentejo, Vinho Verde). Graves supérieures and Haut-Montravel lack `sweet`; Chianti Classico and Crémant de Die carry a style the source gives to another wine.
- **Pouilly-Loché (#2)** renders no facts: its lien is 255 characters (the eAmbrosia register holds an 8,261-character cahier, already queued in CURATOR_TODO). The summary shown instead is the decree boilerplate, correct but empty.

other 24, mistranslation 14, untranslated-term 10, added-qualifier 7, duplicate 7, tautology 5, sibling-or-subzone-confusion 1, hedge-dropped-or-changed 1, narrowed-attribution 1, misfiled-subsection 1, invented-detail 1.

Ten bullets cite their own source in the reader-facing text ("as described on Wikipedia", "Wikipedia places this around 800 mm"): languedoc-sommieres#4, cadillac#4, ventoux#7, romanee-saint-vivant#14, cahors#7, gres-de-montpellier#2, tarragona#9, mergelland#7, criots-batard-montrachet#2, sierras-de-malaga#5, terre-alfieri#1. ### #8 bourgogne-cote-d-or
